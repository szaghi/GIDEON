# Operators

332 explicit kernels, 40 3-D operators and their run-time dispatchers (64 for the 1-D kernels, 8 for the 3-D
operators), 29 compact operators and the line solvers they need, all generated by `scripts/gen_gideon.py` from rows
derived in exact rational arithmetic.

## How a row is derived

A row is the set of weights `w_o` that makes the operator exact on every polynomial its window can determine:

$$ \sum_o w_o\, L_o(x^j) = T(x^j), \qquad j = 0, \dots, N-1, $$

with `L_o` the datum at offset `o` (a point value, or the average over a cell) and `T` the target (the n-th derivative
at a point, or its average over a cell), solved in `fractions.Fraction`. The row's exact degree `K` is then measured on
the monomials beyond `N`, and its order is `K + 1 − n`. Nothing is copied from a table: ADAM's committed tables are
parsed only to cross-check the derived rows, and all 93 non-zero ones agree.

Each row is checked three ways before it is written: its exactness (by construction, then re-measured), its order on
a generic smooth function in 100-digit decimal arithmetic (free of round-off; the smallest margin against the bound
`design − 0.25` is −0.002, `fv_face_ur9`), and, for the left-side upwind rows, that they are the mirrors of the right
ones. The Fortran text is read back and compared with the derived rows. The pipeline: [The generator](/guide/generator).

The orders of every operator, with the points of each window, are tabulated in
[Choose an operator](/cookbook/choose#the-derivative-and-the-order-are-two-numbers); the ghost cells each needs in
[Ghost cells for a window](/cookbook/ghosts).

The FD face operators read point values at $x = o - 1/2$ and return the value or the derivative at the face $x = 0$:
`gideon_fd_face_d1_c4` is FLUME's viscous row, $\big(27(W_1 - W_0) - (W_2 - W_{-1})\big)/24\,\Delta x$. A one-sided
face value (`ur`, `ul`) extrapolates by half a step from the points on one side, as a boundary closure or a ghost value
needs; an upwind-biased one (`bl`, `br`) interpolates from $k + 1$ points on the upwind side and $k$ on the other, the
linear upwind reconstruction of point values ($k = 1$: $(-q_{-1} + 6 q_0 + 3 q_1)/8$, third order).

A centred FV cell derivative is the flux difference of the face row of the derivative below,
`(g(+1/2) − g(−1/2))/ds^n`, computed inside the kernel; an upwind one the same on one side. A combination without an
exact row of order at least 1 has no kernel: a missing symbol, never a row of zeros (ADAM's audit item C).

The 3-D operators take ADAM's FNL signature: one contiguous stencil per direction and component, gathered by the
caller (`vy_x` is the y component along x), so no non-contiguous section enters a device routine. They sum the 1-D
kernels in ADAM's order and reproduce ADAM's values bitwise ([API](/api/#_3-d-operators)).

## Dissipation and the upwind–central split

An undivided odd difference at the face, $\delta^{2k-1} u_{i+1/2}$, is the flux of a conservative dissipation of order
$2k$: its flux difference is $\delta^{2k} u_i \sim h^{2k} u^{(2k)}$. `gideon_<data>_face_diss_c<2k>` returns it, no step
argument, on a window `q(1-k:k)` (the binomial row, the same on point values and on cell averages: proven by the
generator). `gideon_fv_face_diss_c2` and `gideon_fv_face_diss_c4` are the second and fourth differences of
Jameson, Schmidt and Turkel's blend ([Dissipation dialled by a sensor](/cookbook/dissipation)).

Every upwind-biased face value of order $2k+1$ **is** the centred value of order $2k+2$ plus a multiple of one face
difference, proven in exact rationals for both data:

$$ \texttt{face\_bl}_{2k+1} = \texttt{face\_c}_{2k+2} + C_k\, \delta^{2k+1}, \qquad
\texttt{face\_br}_{2k+1} = \texttt{face\_c}_{2k+2} - C_k\, \delta^{2k+1}, $$

with $C_k$ = 1/16, −3/256, 5/2048, −35/65536 on point values (`FD_BDISS_S1` … `FD_BDISS_S4`) and 1/12, −1/60, 1/280,
−1/1260 on cell averages (`FV_BDISS_S1` … `FV_BDISS_S4`; −1/60 is the familiar split of the linear WENO5 value). The
term $\pm C_k \delta^{2k+1}$ alone is `gideon_<data>_face_diss_bl<p>` / `_br<p>`, so a hybrid computes
`centred + sigma * dissipation`: the central scheme at $\sigma = 0$ (bitwise), the biased one at $\sigma = 1$, a
sensor in between. The one-sided rows (`ur`, `ul`) do **not** split this way beyond one cell: `ur2` − `c4` is
$[1, -9, 15, -7]/16$, not a multiple of $\delta^3$ (the generator reports it; roadmap #2 claimed otherwise).

## Explicit filters

`gideon_<data>_filter_c<2k>` returns the filtered value at node (cell) 0 from $2k+1$ data: exact on the polynomials of
degree below $2k$, the odd-even mode removed, which leaves one row, the binomial filter
$u - (-1)^k \delta^{2k} u / 4^k$ (`FILTER_S1` … `FILTER_S5`, the same on both data). Applied after each step of a
central scheme it removes the grid-scale mode the scheme cannot damp ([Filtering a central scheme](/cookbook/filters)).
The compact filters of the same orders are sharper ([Compact operators](/guide/compact)).

## Compact operators

A compact operator is a band solved along a line, its right side a kernel like the ones above: Lele's node
derivatives, the compact filters, face values and derivatives from point values and from cell averages, centred and
upwind-biased. The operators, the solvers and the compact–WENO coupling: [Compact operators](/guide/compact).
