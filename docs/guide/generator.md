# The generator

`scripts/gen_gideon.py` is the only author of GIDEON's numerics. It derives every row from its meaning in exact
rational arithmetic, checks it against that meaning and against ADAM, measures its order, and writes the Fortran:
the rows, the kernels, the dispatchers, the 3-D operators, the test registry and the kernel table of
[Operators](/guide/operators). Every generated file starts with `!< Generated by scripts/gen_gideon.py: do not edit`;
a change of a kernel is a change of the generator. Python 3.10 or later, the standard library only.

```bash
python3 scripts/gen_gideon.py --check            # derive, verify, cross-check; write nothing
python3 scripts/gen_gideon.py --check --list     # the same, and list every kernel with its window and order
python3 scripts/gen_gideon.py                    # the same, then write the generated sources
python3 scripts/gen_gideon.py --check --json F   # also write the derived data as JSON (the documentation's figures)
```

The run takes about half a second and ends with its findings:

```text
checked 93 ADAM rows
orders: 288 rows, smallest margin -0.002 (gideon_fv_face_ur9, observed 8.998)
288 kernels derived
```

It exits 1, writing nothing, on any `MISMATCH`, `NOT GENERATED` or `ORDER` finding, and stops on an assertion when a
row fails a structural check (below).

## 1. Geometry

In units of the grid step, the datum at offset `o` is ([Conventions](/guide/conventions)):

| Geometry | Datum `L_o` | Target `T` |
|---|---|---|
| `fd` node | the point value at $x = o$ | the n-th derivative at $x = 0$ |
| `fd` face | the point value at $x = o - 1/2$ | the value at $x = 0$, between nodes 0 and 1 |
| `fv` face | the average over $[o-1, o]$ | the n-th derivative at $x = 0$, between cells 0 and 1 |
| `fv` cell | the average over $[o-1/2, o+1/2]$ | the average of the n-th derivative over cell 0, or the point value at its centre |

`sampler` and `target` evaluate these functionals on the monomial $x^j$ exactly (`mono_point`, `mono_avg`,
`mono_deriv_point`, `mono_deriv_avg`): a point value is $x^j$, an average is the difference of the primitive over
the cell width.

## 2. Derivation

`derive(data, place, kind, n, offsets)` solves the moment system of the window,

$$ \sum_o w_o\, L_o(x^j) = T(x^j), \qquad j = 0, \dots, N-1, $$

$N$ the number of offsets, by Gauss–Jordan elimination in `fractions.Fraction`. Then `exact_degree` applies the row to
the monomials up to $N+4$ and records the largest $K$ on which it is exact; the order is $K + 1 - n$. The order is a
measurement, not a count: it is at least $N - n$, and more when the row is exact beyond its own system (an even
derivative on a centred window: `gideon_fd_d2_c2` reads 3 points at order 2, `gideon_fv_point_c4` 3 at order 4). A
row of no order of at least 1 is dropped; a row exact on fewer monomials than its own system stops the generator.

## 3. The families

`build()` derives every kernel of the release, by family:

| Family | Windows | Derivatives |
|---|---|---|
| `fd_face_c` | `q(1-S:S)`, $S = 1 \dots 5$ | 0 |
| `fd_node_c` | `q(-S:S)`, $S = 1 \dots 5$ | 1–6 |
| `fv_face_c`, `fv_facedn_c` | `q(1-S:S)`, $S = 1 \dots 5$ | 0–5 |
| `fv_cell_c` | `q(-S:S)`: the flux difference of the face row of derivative $n-1$ | 1–6 |
| `fv_face_u`, `fv_facedn_u` | right `q(1:S)`, left `q(1-S:0)`, $S = 1 \dots 9$ | 0–5 |
| `fv_cell_u` | right `q(0:S)`, left `q(-S:0)`: the flux difference of the upwind face row | 1–6 |
| `deconv` | `q(-S:S)`, $S = 1 \dots 5$ | 0 (average → point, point → average) |

The ranges are owner decision D2: ADAM's half-stencils 1 to 5 for the centred families, one-sided widths 1 to 9 and
the sixth derivative for the upwind ones. Along the way each row passes the structural checks that make the kernels'
arithmetic legal:

- **Pair form.** A centred row must be symmetric or antisymmetric about its centre (`check_pairs`); its half-row is
  what `gideon_tables` stores.
- **Mirrors.** Every left-side upwind row is derived independently on its own window and compared with the right-side
  row mirrored, times $(-1)^n$: equal, or the generator stops. Only the right row is stored; the left kernel reads it
  mirrored, so ADAM's audit item A (a row mirrored twice) cannot be written.
- **Flux differences.** A cell derivative is assembled from the face row, $g(+1/2) - g(-1/2)$, as full weights on the
  cells, and its order is measured on those weights (`composite_order`); the mirrored left operator must keep it.

## 4. Cross-check against ADAM

`parse_adam_tables` reads ADAM's committed tables from `notes/adam-reference/adam_fdv_operators_library.F90` into
exact fractions, and `cross_check_adam` compares each non-zero row with the derived one, through ADAM's storage
conventions (its left-side upwind face rows are stored in ascending cell order; its `FVUR`/`FVUL` face-derivative tables multiply the
cells nearest first). The 93 non-zero rows agree. ADAM is a check, never a source: a disagreement is reported
(`MISMATCH`, or `NOT GENERATED` for an ADAM row with no derived counterpart) and fails the run, it is never adopted.
ADAM's zero rows are skipped: they are the unsupported orders of audit item C, absent kernels in GIDEON.

## 5. Orders at 100 digits

`check_orders` applies every row, with its exact weights, to the cell averages or point values of
$g(x) = \sin(1.3x + 0.4) + 0.5\cos(0.7x - 0.2)$ around $x_0 = 0.37$, in 100-digit `decimal` arithmetic, at
$h = 2^{-11}$ and $2^{-12}$, and computes the observed order $\log_2(e_1/e_2)$. Two incommensurate waves at a generic
point: no derivative vanishes, so no leading term cancels by accident. With round-off out of the way, this is the
rows' own order; the frozen bound is `design − 0.25`, the smallest margin is −0.002 (`gideon_fv_face_ur9`, 8.998).
The compiled kernels' orders are measured again by `gideon_test_order`, where each kind's round-off allows
([Precision](/guide/precision)).

## 6. Emission

- **Rows** (`gideon_tables.F90`): one `real(R_P), parameter` per stored row, its literals `p._R_P/q._R_P` (the compiler
  folds the division in the build's kind): 134 rows for 288 kernels, since left-side and cell kernels read the rows of
  their right-side and face twins.
- **Kernels** (`gideon_fd_centred.F90`, `gideon_fv_centred.F90`, `gideon_fv_upwind.F90`,
  `gideon_deconvolution.F90`): `emit_kernel` writes the explicit-shape window, the loop over the compile-time row in
  ADAM's arithmetic shape (pair form, D3; the step divided inside, `d/ds/ds` for `fd` and `d/(ds*ds)` for `fv`, D4),
  `!$acc routine seq` and `!$omp declare target`.
- **Dispatchers** (`gideon_dispatch.F90`): one per operator, `select case` over its orders, `GIDEON_NAN` by default.
  `window_expr` fits the window bounds as expressions of `order` (`order/2 + c` or `order + c`) and checks the
  formula against every kernel of the group.
- **3-D operators** (`gideon_operators_3d.F90`): gradient, divergence, curl and Laplacian of every centred FD and FV
  d1 (d2 for the Laplacian) order, over directional stencils, summed in x, y, z order, and their dispatchers.
- **Test registry** (`src/tests/gideon_test_registry.F90`): every kernel's metadata (geometry, target, derivative,
  order, window, exact degree, $\sum|c|$, the exact leading error on $x^{K+1}$, constant-data property, mirror twin)
  and the routines that apply kernel `id` directly, on the device, through its dispatcher, and through its dispatcher
  at an order without a kernel. The tests loop over it ([Add an operator](/cookbook/new-operator)).
- **Documentation** (`docs/guide/_operators-table.md`): the table of every kernel.

Before writing, the emitted rows are parsed back from the Fortran text and compared with the derived fractions; then
only the files whose text changed are written, so a run that changes nothing touches nothing.
